Spider

Spiders (order Araneae) are air-breathing arthropods that have eight legs, chelicerae with fangs that generally inject venom, and spinnerets that extrude silk. They are the largest order of arachnids and rank seventh in total species diversity among all organisms.

Etymology

Arachnologists have proposed various hypotheses for the etymology of the word "spider" from Old English spíþra, from Proto-Germanic *spinþrōn, connected to the verb spinnan "to spin". The common name spider is derived from spider, referring to the way the creature spins its web.

Body plan

Like other arachnids, the body is divided into two tagmata: the cephalothorax (or prosoma) and the abdomen (or opisthosoma), connected by a narrow waist called the pedicel. The cephalothorax is covered by a single plate, the carapace, and the abdomen by a flexible cuticle.

Spiders have eight legs arranged in four pairs, and the legs are composed of seven segments: coxa, trochanter, femur, patella, tibia, metatarsus and tarsus. Each leg ends in two or three claws and is covered with sensory hairs that detect vibrations and air currents.

Silk

Spider silk is a protein fibre spun by spiders. A spider uses silk to build webs, to wrap and store prey, to line its retreat, to construct egg sacs, and to drift through the air as ballooning silk. Strength-to-weight ratio of dragline silk is comparable to steel and Kevlar.

  1. Orb webs: Araneidae — the classic spiral wheel web
  2. Funnel webs: Agelenidae, built as a flat sheet with a retreat
  3. Cobwebs: Theridiidae, tangled three-dimensional structures

Ecology

All spiders are predators, and most hunt with the aid of silk. A few species actively chase prey, while the majority sit and wait in a retreat, sensing vibrations transmitted through the substrate. Predation rates are high: a single spider may consume hundreds of insects in its lifetime, making them valuable in biological pest control.

Male spiders court females with species-specific signals: vibrations of the web, abdominal tremors, or rhythmic leg waving. After mating, the female spins an egg sac containing hundreds of eggs.

Classification

  • Suborder Mesothelae — Liphistiidae (segmented spiders)
  • Suborder Opisthothelae — Mygalomorphae and Araneomorphae
  • Family Theraphosidae — tarantulas; family Salticidae — jumping spiders
  • Extant Araneae families: Araneidae, Lycosidae, Pholcidae

Fossils of spiders date back to the Devonian Palaeodictyognathus from the Silurian period; the earliest known web-bearing spider lived about 100 million years ago in Cretaceous amber.
